Details — the verbatim abstract

Fetched live this run (HTTP 200) from the GSA OpenWater gallery: https://gsameetings.secure-platform.com/ne26/gallery/rounds/82013/details/18182. Hydrothermal mineral deposits in the Western Uplands of Connecticut have been known and exploited for over 250 years. Although their mineralogies are well-known, only the hydrothermal vein systems genetically associated with the Permian Pinewood intrusive suite have a robust genetic model. This Cornubian-type system of 3 plutons and swarms of hydrothermal fissure veins form a trend of about 315 degrees over 10 km in northern Trumbull paralleling the Alleghenian tectonic convergence vector. The Pinewoodian genetic model suggests a local northwestern deepening of the Alleghenian plutons responsible for its hypothermal fissure veins.This study examines the potential for a more distal continuation of that model based on an additional Permian syenite stock in Southbury, a number of undated quartz-rich mineral fissure veins situated as far as Roxbury, 3 quartz masses, and an albite-quartz-sulfide deposit of unknown genesis. The Pinewoodian and undated (fissure) veins all have northwest-orientated, parallel, smooth-walled, unbrecciated host-rock contacts and nearly solid quartz compositions. This is in contrast to presumably Mesozoic hydrothermal veins in the same region that are brecciated, vuggy and exhibit mixed calcite-quartz-fluorite-barite-sulfide-zeolite mineralogy. Five minerals – topaz, pyrrhotite, bismuth, arsenopyrite and siderite, chosen as indexes of different vein crystallization temperatures, show a progressive change from hypothermal in the southeast to mesothermal/epithermal in the northwest. This pattern serves as a proxy for a corresponding depth increase of their potential Alleghenian source plutons.

6. What it means for the case study

The abstract extends the Pinewood tungsten system ~10 km NW to Roxbury via a Southbury syenite stock, along a ~315° trend paralleling Alleghanian convergence — the system is a regional metallogenic zone, not a Trumbull local.

Binding consequence for the playbook: any watershed-scale W-anomaly search (NURE context, playbook item 4) should extend NW along the ~315° Pinewood trend, not just around the park. The state database does not yet record the newly-named sites — the abstract is ahead of the database.
